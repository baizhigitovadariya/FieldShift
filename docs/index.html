Where your judgment matters most

  • These are county averages. One field can be well above or below, depending on soil, slope, drainage, rotation and seeding date. You know your fields. This tool does not.
  • Sales per acre is not profit. Corn and sunflower cost more to grow than wheat or peas. Seed, fertilizer, drying, hauling and insurance are not counted here.
  • A dry season here is an average of four dry years. A drought that is also very hot, like 2021, can hit much harder. Check the worst reported year on each crop.
  • Rotation, disease and herbicide history can rule a crop out no matter what the numbers say.
  • Talk it through with your agronomist, your county Extension agent and your elevator before changing a plan.

How far to trust the numbers

The typical number is the county's middle reported yield for 2020 to 2025. The dry and wet change comes from a weather model. The model was tested on years it never saw. Here is how far off it was, next to simply using each county's own average.

Lower is better. Bold marks the smaller miss. The model helps most for canola, durum and sunflower, and does not help for soybeans. Wet season numbers are the least certain.
